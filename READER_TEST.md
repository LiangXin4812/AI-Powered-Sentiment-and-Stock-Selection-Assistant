# Reader comprehension check (not yet conducted)

Purpose: test whether the Daily Signal Report lets a beginner understand a
trading period quickly and know how far to trust each statement, without
relying on trading returns. Recruit at least three readers who did not build
the system. Use two or three dated reports, including one with a withheld
forecast and one in which the stored evidence does not explain a large move.
Fix the dates before collecting responses; do not show answers in advance.

For each report, ask the reader to:

1. state the period covered, the average move of the 15 stocks and the
   largest move;
2. say whether the report claims to know why the largest move happened, and
   name one thing it suggests checking;
3. explain what a withheld three-day forecast means;
4. explain what "likely quieter than most" means and what it does not mean
   (it is not a statement that a stock is safe and gives no direction);
5. explain the typical range (about one in three past changes went beyond it).

Score each item 0 (incorrect), 1 (partly correct) or 2 (correct). Record
completion time, wording that confused the reader, and any reading of a range
or a "likely quieter" call as a forecast of direction or a guarantee. Ask what
they would verify next, without soliciting a trade recommendation.

Report the dated cases, participant count, individual score distribution and
wording changes. A small convenience sample assesses usability and
comprehension; it cannot establish investment performance or representative
investor outcomes. Do not claim the test is complete until responses have been
collected.
